DESCRIPTION
Dorf Ketal Tyzor® organic titanates comprise a series of titanium
and zirconate esters ranging from very reactive Tyzor® TPT
(tetraisopropyl titanate) to stabilized chelated forms such as Tyzor®
LA, a lactic acid chelate.  Members of this titanate series vary in
stability and solubility characteristics, as well as reactivity.
Titanates are proving useful in a wide range of industries.  
For example, they are used as polymerization catalysts with
polyester, phenolic, silicone and epoxy resins.  In these
applications, titanates provide rapid cures at relatively low
temperatures and often improve hardness, solvent resistance 
and electrical properties.
Titanates will hydrolyze to deposit a thin film of titanium dioxide 
on a variety of surfaces.  In different applications, these thin films of
titanium dioxide can impart adhesion-promotion effects to foils and
films, scratch resistance on glass, improved electrical properties 
on electronic components, and aid in pigment dispersion.
Bulk handling of these products is frequently attractive, and this
document provides general information concerning the bulk
storage and handling of Tyzor® TPT.  
SHIPPING CONTAINERS
Phosphated, unlined steel drums are normally used for small 
unit shipments.  These must be purged with dry air or nitrogen
before loading because atmospheric humidity will cause product
hydrolysis.

DESCRIPTION
Dorf Ketal Tyzor® organic titanates are valuable, environmentally
friendly catalysts for the direct esterification of aromatic and
aliphatic mono, di, tri, and other carboxylic acids to make a variety
of esters, some of which are used as plasticizers.
Plasticizers are important polymer additives that improve
processing and enhance desirable product properties such as
flexibility and toughness.  Although epoxidized vegetable oils,
phosphates and polymeric plasticizers are used, most plasticizers
are diesters of phthalic acid that contain alkoxy groups such as
butylates, n-octylates, iso-octylates and 2-ethylhexylates used to
modify the properties of polyvinyl chloride (PVC). 
The most common example is di-2-ethyl hexyl phthalate (also
called Di Octyl Phthalate, abbreviated DOP).  This phthalate is
typically prepared by esterification of phthalic anhydride (PA) with
corresponding alcohols, but other commonly used di- and
tricarboxylate plasticizers can also be made by esterification of
acids with alcohols. 
Esterification of phthalic anhydride and other di- and tricarboxylic
acids to plasticizers requires an esterification catalyst, and Tyzor®
organic titanate catalysts are popular choices due to their many
performance advantages.

DESCRIPTION
Dorf Ketal Tyzor® organic titanates are excellent catalysts in a
number of chemical reactions.  They are particularly useful in
esterifications and transesterifications because the catalysis is
accomplished with a minimum of undesirable side reactions and

aldol condensations and in Meerwein-Ponndorf-Verley reductions.

organic titanates is the quality of the excess alcohol recovered from
the reactions.  Acid-catalyzed methods often cause extensive
degradation of the alcohol during the reaction, but titanate catalysis
avoids this, allowing recycling of recovered alcohols.

DESCRIPTION
Dorf Ketal Tyzor® organic titanates and zirconates are used to
prevent glass surfaces from scratching and to provide adhesion
and bonding of other metal oxide finishes to glass and other
surfaces with similar characteristics.  
When a glass surface is treated at a temperature between its strain
and deforming temperatures with the vapor of an organic titanate
ester or chelate, the titanate pyrolyzes to form a (TiO2)X coating on
the glass surface.
APPLICATIONS
A thin (<100 nm) film of (TiO2)X is virtually transparent.  These films
are believed to be bound to the glass surface by Ti-O-Si bonds.
After a lubricant is applied, these bonds impart considerable
scratch resistance to the glass and consequently greatly reduce 
its fragility.
Tyzor® TPT is generally preferred for glass treatment, but chelates
such as Tyzor® AA-75, TE, and PITA also have been used.  They
can be applied undiluted, as a vapor,1-3 or in a solvent such as ethyl
acetate,4,5 kerosene,6 silicone fluid,7 or benzyl alcohol.8

uniform coating on the glass surface. Improvements in the

DESCRIPTION
Dorf Ketal Tyzor® organic titanates make excellent primers for the
treatment of a variety of metals, plastics, and even fluoropolymers
to accept paints, inks, rubber-based coatings and many others 
too numerous to mention.
METALS
To use Tyzor® organic titanates as a primer, dilute to a
concentration of 0.1 to 2% and apply as a thin coating to the
surface of the substrate.  As the solvent evaporates, atmospheric
moisture hydrolyzes the titanate to form a thin, reactive film of
polymeric TiO2.  This film bonds tenaciously to the substrate and 
to the subsequent coating through functional groups containing
active hydrogen.  Usually the more reactive alkoxy titanates, such
as Tyzor® TPT and TNBT, are used; however, chelated titanates 
can be used if heat is available to accelerate the hydrolysis.  If the
solution is applied too heavily or dried in an atmosphere too high in
humidity, the titanate may powder on the surface rather than form 
a continuous film.  A relative humidity of 30-60% is recommended.
Titanates are used as primers for coatings such as paints, inks, 
and silicone rubbers on steel, aluminum, zinc, and copper to
promote adhesion and to improve resistance to corrosion, water,
and discoloration.1-11

DESCRIPTION
Dorf Ketal Tyzor® TPT freezes at 18 °C (64 °F), which is very close
to room temperature, so it may become solid during shipment or
storage.  Because Tyzor® TPT does not expand while freezing,
containers will not bulge or show signs that the product has frozen.  
Tyzor® TPT is 100% tetraisopropyl titanate and mixing is not
necessary to obtain a homogeneous product when thawing.  
If frozen, Tyzor® TPT can be melted or thawed by careful, 
controlled heating.  Overheating beyond the recommended
temperature or prolonged storage at the limit of the recommended
thawing temperature can cause the product to yellow significantly.
Tyzor® TPT is adversely affected by atmospheric moisture.  
The product will react irreversibly with moisture to produce
isopropyl alcohol and hydrous titanium dioxide.  
CAUTION
The flash point of Tyzor® TPT can decrease to <-6.67 °C (20 °F)
when exposed to moisture because isopropyl alcohol is liberated
during the reaction of Tyzor® TPT with water.
